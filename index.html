About Me

Bridging quantum computing and real-world AI.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical Engineering at Chung Yuan Christian University (CYCU), Taiwan (expected December 2026), advised by Prof. Ying-Yi Hong. My dissertation is on quantum artificial intelligence for resilient power systems: advanced spatiotemporal forecasting of renewable energy and extreme weather events.

I build end-to-end forecasting pipelines on enterprise NVIDIA A100/H100 HPC clusters and cloud infrastructures using CUDA-Q, cuQuantum, PyTorch, and Lightning, and I have deployed optimized computer vision and NLP models for real-world commercial applications.

I am an NVIDIA Academic Grant recipient and co-author with the NVIDIA AI Technology Center (NVAITC). I have led multidisciplinary research initiatives, published in top-tier journals, and mentored engineering teams and students across academic and corporate environments.

  • HPC and optimization: GPU-accelerated pipelines (up to 109× speedup) and swarm/evolutionary metaheuristics for model tuning.

Adaptive Diversity-Enhanced Phasor Particle Swarm Optimization–Differential Evolution Hybrid for Unimodal–Multimodal Problems with Engineering Applications
Machine Learning with Applications, vol. 25, pp. 1–25, July 2026
C. L. P. P. Rioflorido & Y.-Y. Hong · DOI: 10.1016/j.mlwa.2026.100955
Abstract

Proposes CQPPSO-DE, a hybrid metaheuristic that pairs Phasor Particle Swarm Optimization (PPSO) with success-history adaptive Differential Evolution (DE). An ablation study on 64 benchmark functions shows both components are essential: adding DE to the PPSO baseline gave statistically significant gains on 59 of the 64 problems. Two secondary mechanisms refine performance on specific landscapes: Lorenz chaotic initialization improves initial population diversity (most helpful on unimodal and separable problems), and a quantum-inspired random walk adds targeted perturbation on multimodal problems where PPSO tends to converge prematurely. Evaluated on the CEC2021 and CEC2022 suites and six engineering design problems, CQPPSO-DE is competitive with state-of-the-art metaheuristics.

Hybrid Temporal Fusion Transformer and Quantum Long Short-Term Memory Network for Typhoon Forecasting at a Wind Power Farm
Energy, vol. 360, pp. 1–13, July 2026
Y.-Y. Hong, C. L. P. P. Rioflorido, Y.-Y. Wang, T.-C. Huang, S. See, H.-P. Van, & M.-T. Li · DOI: 10.1016/j.energy.2026.141900
Abstract

Proposes the Temporal Fusion Transformer–Quantum Long Short-Term Memory (TFT-QLSTM) network to improve forecasting for wind farms during typhoons, with a case study of the Gaomei wind farm in Taichung. The framework produces joint 24-hour-ahead forecasts of seven typhoon and wind farm variables, is optimized with Chaos Quantum Energy Valley Optimization (CQEVO), and remains interpretable through the TFT's variable selection network.

A Hybrid Graph-Quantum Neural Network for Solar Irradiance Forecasting Using Chaotic Swarm Optimization and Cauchy Distribution
IEEE Transactions on Power Systems, pp. 1619–1631, May 2026
Y.-Y. Hong, C. L. P. P. Rioflorido, & Y.-Y. Wang · DOI: 10.1109/TPWRS.2025.3626336
Abstract

Presents a 24-hour-ahead global horizontal irradiance (GHI) forecasting framework for power system applications. A Hybrid Graph-Quantum Neural Network (HGQNN) combines graph convolution networks and quantum neural networks to capture spatial and temporal relationships in solar irradiance data and to quantify forecast uncertainty. The architecture and hyperparameters are tuned with a proposed Chaotic Phasor Particle Swarm Optimization with Cauchy Distribution (CPPSO-CD). Simulations show better accuracy than traditional approaches such as back-propagation neural networks, CNNs, LSTMs, and self-attention-based models.

Hybrid Deep Learning and Quantum-Inspired Neural Network for Day-Ahead Spatiotemporal Wind Speed Forecasting
Expert Systems with Applications, vol. 241, pp. 1–11, May 2024
Y.-Y. Hong, C. L. P. P. Rioflorido, & W. Zhang · DOI: 10.1016/j.eswa.2023.122645
Abstract

Temporal Fusion Transformer Integrated with Grey Wolf Optimizer for 24-hour Ahead Load Forecasting
Preprints of the 23rd IFAC World Congress, Busan, Republic of Korea, Aug. 23–28, 2026
Y.-Y. Hong, C. L. P. P. Rioflorido, C. M. Chen, C. J. Yang, J. M. F. Centeno, H. R. Limjoco, L. V. Gulmatico, M. J. N. L. Manalo, & J. L. C. Onia
Abstract

This paper proposes a 24-hour ahead load forecasting framework that integrates the Temporal Fusion Transformer (TFT) with the Grey Wolf Optimizer (GWO) to address forecasting challenges in Taiwan’s power system, which is affected by renewable energy integration, industrial variability, and extreme weather. The TFT component captures multi-scale temporal dependencies and provides interpretability, while the GWO is employed for systematic hyperparameter optimization to improve accuracy and robustness. The framework is validated using nine years (2017–2025) of Taiwan Power Company operational data, including load profiles, meteorological variables, and economic indicators. Compared with benchmark models, the proposed TFT-GWO achieves superior results, with MAE (mean absolute error), RMSE (root mean squared error), and R2 (coefficient of determination) of 0.0145, 0.0220, and 0.9390, respectively. The results demonstrate that the proposed approach supports more reliable unit commitment, reserve allocation, and day-ahead market operations, highlighting the effectiveness of combining transformer-based architectures with metaheuristic optimization in power system forecasting.

Classification of Photovoltaic Faults using CatBoost Classifier Optimized by QPSO
2nd International Conference on "Green Solutions and Emerging Technologies for Sustainability" (GSETS 2025), Ho Chi Minh City, Vietnam, pp. 297–308
Y.-Y. Hong, C. L. P. P. Rioflorido, & H.-P. Van
Abstract

Photovoltaic (PV) systems are an essential source of renewable energy generation, but their performance is sensitive to a combination of component faults. Thus, the reliability of PV systems can be ensured with the assistance of fault detection and classification. This work presents a new technique for fault classification using CatBoost, a machine learning algorithm that lies in a family of gradient boosting decision trees (GBDT), which is chosen owing to its performance with complex data sets and categorical features. However, its classification performance is dependent on the hyperparameter setting of the model. This paper implements Quantum Particle Swarm Optimization (QPSO) algorithm to optimize hyperparameter values. The hybrid technique proposed is referred to as CatBoost-QPSO and is applied to a dataset that classifies 10 fault types: line-to-line faults, module open-circuit and short-circuit faults in a string and both strings, partial shading conditions in a string and both strings and normal operating conditions. To assess the performance of the CatBoost-QPSO procedure, various benchmark metrics (accuracy, precision, recall, and F1 score) are utilized. The proposed method achieves an accuracy of 93.27%, which indicates that the method could be efficient and robust to compute. These findings underscore the significance of leveraging both advanced boosting techniques with hyper-optimization methods for fault classification towards enhancing PV systems. This research found a new way to increase the reliability and operational efficiency of the renewable energy infrastructure, which will cut down the downtime and enable maximum energy generation.

A Deep CNN-LSTM Model Tuned by PPSO for Spatiotemporal Solar GHI Forecasting
2023 IEEE PES Innovative Smart Grid Technologies Europe (ISGT EUROPE), Grenoble, France, Oct. 2023, pp. 1–5
Y.-Y. Hong & C. L. P. P. Rioflorido · DOI: 10.1109/ISGTEUROPE56780.2023.10407345
Abstract

Proposes a deep learning model that combines a convolutional neural network and an LSTM for 24-hour-ahead solar GHI forecasting. It uses spatiotemporal information, capturing spatial and temporal dependencies from multiple locations and time windows. Phasor particle swarm optimization (PPSO), a PSO variant that represents particles with complex numbers, tunes the number of layers, filters, and neurons. Design of a Non-Invasive Egg Sexing Device Utilizing Artificial Intelligence for Duck Species
TENCON 2023 — IEEE Region 10 Conference, Chiang Mai, Thailand, Oct. 31 – Nov. 3, 2023, pp. 1169–1174
S. U. Arenas, P. J. R. Billones, J. C. V. Lao, J. A. C. Mercado, I. D. G. Sy, & C. L. P. P. Rioflorido · DOI: 10.1109/TENCON58879.2023.10322315
Abstract

Introduces a non-invasive device that combines AI, spectroscopy, and computer vision to determine the sex of duck embryos inside eggs. Plumage color serves as the sex indicator, and a hybrid CNN and gated recurrent unit (GRU) model works alongside the spectroscopic analysis. Experiments report 98.68% sexing accuracy with an average processing time of about 37 ms. Hatchability stayed at 74.80% across a population of 500 eggs, indicating that spectroscopy does not harm egg viability. The work offers a more sustainable way to manage male ducklings in the industry.

A Short-Term Spatiotemporal Wind Speed Forecasting Using CNN Optimized by PSO
2022 IET International Conference on Engineering Technologies and Applications (IET-ICETA), Changhua, Taiwan, Oct. 14–16, 2022, pp. 1–2
Y.-Y. Hong, J. B. D. Santos, S.-S. Lin, Y.-D. Lee, J.-H. Wang, J.-L. Jiang, C. L. P. P. Rioflorido, & J.-N. Yeh · DOI: 10.1109/IET-ICETA56553.2022.9971559
Abstract

Since precise wind speed forecasts can be converted directly into accurate wind power predictions, wind speed forecasting is essential for the operation of power systems. A spatiotemporal wind speed prediction model based on the 2D Convolutional Neural Network (2D CNN) optimized by the Particle Swarm optimization (PSO) is proposed in this paper. In order for the 2D CNN to establish parameters adaptively, the hyperparameters (kernel size and length, dropout rate, and the number of input cells in the hidden layers) are optimized by PSO. Five wind speed datasets in Taiwan were used for this study. The results validate the applicability of the proposed method.

Soft Sensing Measurement of Dissolved Ammonia Nitrogen in Tank-Based Eel Aquaculture Systems Utilizing Deep Learning
2022 IET International Conference on Engineering Technologies and Applications (IET-ICETA), Changhua, Taiwan, Oct. 14–16, 2022
D. J. A. Amora, J. V. Apan, D. N. C. Layosa, R. C. Sarmiento, J. J. Tugado, & C. L. P. P. Rioflorido · DOI: 10.1109/IET-ICETA56553.2022.9971667
Abstract

Japanese eels are susceptible to low concentrations of dissolved ammonia nitrogen (NH3−N). To provide proper intervention, measuring the dissolved ammonia nitrogen in the tank-based eel aquaculture is crucial. However, existing laboratory methods and digital sensors are time-consuming and expensive solutions. This study proposed an Attentive Interpretable Tabular Learning (TabNet) Deep Learning soft-sensing model to predict dissolved NH3-N concentrations. The prediction model is based on input parameters: water temperature, conductivity, pH, and dissolved oxygen. The datasets are gathered from the Global Environment Monitoring System (GEMStat), a water quality parameter database. The performance of the proposed Deep Learning model was compared with Feed-forward neural network (F-NN) and One Dimensional-CNN. The models were evaluated using statistical methods: R-squared (R2), Mean Absolute Error (MAE), and Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE). The simulation results showed that TabNet performed better in predicting dissolved NH3-N concentrations.

Design of a Household Consumption Based Water Leak Detection System Utilizing Machine Learning Algorithm
2022 IET International Conference on Engineering Technologies and Applications (IET-ICETA), Changhua, Taiwan, Oct. 14–16, 2022
D. J. A. Amora, M. A. V. Janapin, B. A. M. Calayag, C. L. P. P. Rioflorido, N. M. Estur, & P. A. L. Villegas · DOI: 10.1109/IET-ICETA56553.2022.9971564
Abstract

Water is a limited resource; thus, every drop matters. Leaks in water distribution networks (WDNs) and residences contribute considerably to water utility losses. The goal of this research is to design and build a water leak-detecting system based on home use. Based on historical water consumption and experimental flow rate water consumption data, this research developed a Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) machine learning (ML) approach for building a water leakage detection model. The BiLSTM model is compared against Gated recurrent units (GRU) and Autoregressive models on the following performance metrics: mean squared error (MSE), root mean squared error (RMSE), mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), and mean absolute error (MAE). According to the data, the BiLSTM model performed better overall in water leak detection.
